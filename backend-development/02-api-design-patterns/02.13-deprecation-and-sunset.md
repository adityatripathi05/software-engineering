# 02.13 API Deprecation, Compatibility and Sunset

> **Prerequisites:** 02.3 (versioning), 02.4 (the contract as an artefact), 02.9 (error types are permanent)
> **What you'll learn:**
> - Run a deprecation as a **migration programme** with evidence, not as an announcement with a date
> - Emit `Deprecation` and `Sunset` headers (RFC 9745, RFC 8594) that tooling can act on
> - Use brownouts to find the clients your telemetry and your outreach both missed
> - Decide removal from usage data rather than from the calendar
> **Level:** Intermediate

---

## Concept

### Plain-English Explanation

Publishing an API is easy. Un-publishing one is where the real cost of the promise arrives.

The instinct is to treat deprecation as a communication task: write a changelog entry, email
customers, pick a date six months out, and switch it off. That fails reliably, for a reason worth
stating plainly: **the people you email are not the people who wrote the integration.** Your contact
is in finance or procurement. The engineer who wrote the code left the company two years ago. Nobody
at that customer reads your changelog, and nothing in their monitoring says "you are calling a
deprecated endpoint".

So a deprecation that works is not a message. It is a programme with three parts: measure who is
actually using it, make the deprecation visible *in the channel the client already reads* (their HTTP
responses, their IDE, their logs), and remove it only when the usage data says nobody is left. The
date is an output of that process, not an input to it.

### Technical Explanation

**The two headers**, both now standardised:

```http
HTTP/1.1 200 OK
Deprecation: @1756032422                                     ← RFC 9745; when it BECAME deprecated
Sunset: Sat, 28 Feb 2027 23:59:59 GMT                        ← RFC 8594; when it will STOP working
Link: <https://docs.ledgerly.com/migrations/v1-to-v2>; rel="deprecation"; type="text/html",
      <https://api.ledgerly.com/v2/invoices>; rel="successor-version"
Warning: 299 - "This endpoint is deprecated; migrate to /v2 before 2027-02-28."
```

- **`Deprecation`** — an `@`-prefixed Unix timestamp of when deprecation took effect (or `true` in
  older usage). It says *this still works, but it is on the way out*.
- **`Sunset`** — an HTTP-date after which the resource is expected to become unavailable. It is a
  commitment; do not emit a date you are unwilling to keep or unwilling to move.
- **`Link` with `rel="deprecation"`** — where to read about it. `rel="successor-version"` points at
  the replacement.
- **`Warning: 299`** — legacy but still surfaced by some HTTP clients and proxies; harmless to include.

These matter because they are **machine-readable in the channel the client is already reading**.
Linters, SDKs and gateways can surface them without anyone reading email. Ledgerly's SDKs log a
warning on every deprecated response, which is how a lot of engineers first learn about it.

**OpenAPI carries the same information into tooling** (02.4):

```yaml
paths:
  /v1/invoices:
    get:
      deprecated: true
      description: |
        **Deprecated.** Use `/v2/invoices`. Sunset: 2027-02-28.
        Migration guide: https://docs.ledgerly.com/migrations/v1-to-v2
```

`deprecated: true` produces strikethrough in generated documentation and deprecation warnings in
generated SDKs — so an engineer's editor tells them, at the moment they write the call.

**Usage telemetry is the part that cannot be retrofitted.** You need, per deprecated surface:

```text
which API key · which tenant · which route · how often · when last seen
```

Without it you cannot tell who to contact, cannot tell whether outreach worked, and cannot tell when
removal is safe. Ledgerly learned this the hard way (below), and it is the single most important
prerequisite in this notebook — you must be collecting it *before* you deprecate anything.

**Brownouts** are scheduled, short, pre-announced outages of the deprecated surface:

```text
2026-11-15  10:00–10:15 UTC   deprecated endpoints return 410 Gone
2026-12-13  10:00–11:00 UTC   1 hour
2027-01-17  10:00–14:00 UTC   4 hours
2027-02-28                    permanent removal
```

Their purpose is to convert a *future* failure into a *present* one, at a time you have chosen and
with staff watching. Clients you never identified — because they use a shared key, or a key registered
to a contact who left — surface as support tickets during a window when you can help them, rather than
at 2 a.m. on the removal date.

### Mental Model

> **You cannot remove what you cannot measure.** A deprecation is a programme with three phases —
> *announce, measure, verify* — and removal is what happens when usage reaches zero. The date is the
> output of the process; treating it as the input is how you take a customer's production down on a
> Tuesday morning.

## How It Works

The full lifecycle, with the gate that matters marked:

```text
  T-12 months   ANNOUNCE
                ├─ successor shipped and documented; migration guide published
                ├─ Deprecation + Sunset + Link headers on every deprecated response
                ├─ deprecated: true in OpenAPI → SDK and IDE warnings
                └─ email BOTH the billing contact AND every technical contact
                          │
  T-9 to T-3    MEASURE AND CONTACT
                ├─ per-key usage dashboard; weekly review
                ├─ targeted outreach to the top N users, with their own numbers
                │  ("your key key_live_2b8e made 41,000 v1 calls last week")
                └─ offer migration help to the largest accounts
                          │
  T-3 months    BROWNOUTS  15 min → 1 hour → 4 hours
                └─ each one surfaces clients that telemetry and outreach missed
                          │
  T-0           ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                │ GATE: is usage zero for 30 consecutive days?          │
                │   yes → remove, return 410 Gone with a Link           │
                │   no  → DO NOT REMOVE. Extend, and contact who is left│
                └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

That gate is the whole discipline. A date on a slide does not migrate anyone; a dashboard showing one
customer still making 41,000 calls a week is the thing that stops you shipping an outage.

**After removal, return `410 Gone`, never `404`.** `410` means "this existed and is deliberately
gone" — which tells an engineer debugging it that they need a migration, not that they have a typo.
Include the `Link` to the migration guide in the problem body (02.9).

## Code Example

Marking a route deprecated, with the headers and the telemetry as one decorator:

```python
# ledgerly/deprecation.py
from datetime import datetime, UTC
from functools import wraps
from typing import Callable

from fastapi import Response
from prometheus_client import Counter

DEPRECATED_USAGE = Counter(
    "deprecated_endpoint_requests_total",
    "Requests to deprecated endpoints — the data that gates removal",
    ["route", "api_key_id", "tenant_id"],
)

def deprecated(*, since: datetime, sunset: datetime, guide: str, successor: str):
    """Emits RFC 9745 / RFC 8594 headers AND records who is still calling.

    The headers are useless without the telemetry: they tell clients, but only
    the counter tells YOU whether it is safe to remove."""
    def decorator(handler: Callable):
        @wraps(handler)
        async def wrapper(*args, response: Response, **kwargs):
            response.headers["Deprecation"] = f"@{int(since.timestamp())}"
            response.headers["Sunset"] = sunset.strftime("%a, %d %b %Y %H:%M:%S GMT")
            response.headers["Link"] = (
                f'<{guide}>; rel="deprecation"; type="text/html", '
                f'<{successor}>; rel="successor-version"'
            )
            response.headers["Warning"] = (
                f'299 - "Deprecated; migrate to {successor} before '
                f'{sunset:%Y-%m-%d}."'
            )
            return await handler(*args, response=response, **kwargs)
        return wrapper
    return decorator
```

⚠️ Metric label cardinality: `api_key_id` and `tenant_id` are unbounded in principle (15.3). Ledgerly
accepts it here because deprecated routes are low-volume by definition and the data is worthless
aggregated — you need to know *which* key, not how many. Bound it by dropping the labels once a route
is removed.

Applied to a route, with `deprecated=True` so it reaches OpenAPI and every generated SDK:

```python
# ledgerly/routers/v1/invoices.py
from datetime import datetime, UTC

SINCE = datetime(2026, 2, 28, tzinfo=UTC)
SUNSET = datetime(2027, 2, 28, 23, 59, 59, tzinfo=UTC)

@router.get("", response_model=list[InvoiceOutV1], name="listInvoicesV1",
            deprecated=True,                      # → OpenAPI, docs, SDK warnings (02.4)
            description="**Deprecated.** Use `/v2/invoices`. Sunset: 2027-02-28. "
                        "See https://docs.ledgerly.com/migrations/v1-to-v2")
@deprecated(since=SINCE, sunset=SUNSET,
            guide="https://docs.ledgerly.com/migrations/v1-to-v2",
            successor="https://api.ledgerly.com/v2/invoices")
async def list_invoices_v1(response: Response, svc: Service,
                           tenant: Tenant) -> list[InvoiceOutV1]:
    return [InvoiceOutV1.from_domain(i) for i in await svc.list_invoices(tenant)]
```

The brownout, driven by a feature flag so it can be aborted in seconds (14.16):

```python
# ledgerly/middleware/brownout.py
async def brownout_middleware(request: Request, call_next):
    """Scheduled, announced, short unavailability of a deprecated surface.
    Its purpose is to surface clients that telemetry and outreach did not find,
    at a time when engineers are watching and can help them."""
    if is_deprecated_path(request.url.path) and await flags.enabled("v1_brownout"):
        logger.warning("brownout_rejection", extra={
            "path": request.url.path,
            "api_key_id": getattr(request.state, "api_key_id", None),
            "tenant_id": getattr(request.state, "tenant_id", None),
        })   # ← every rejection identifies a client we did not know about
        return JSONResponse(
            status_code=410, media_type="application/problem+json",
            headers={"Link": '<https://docs.ledgerly.com/migrations/v1-to-v2>; '
                             'rel="deprecation"'},
            content={"type": "https://api.ledgerly.com/problems/api-version-sunset",
                     "title": "API version sunset", "status": 410,
                     "detail": "This is a scheduled brownout of API v1, which will be "
                               "removed on 2027-02-28. Migrate to v2.",
                     "sunset": "2027-02-28"},
        )
    return await call_next(request)
```

The removal gate, run as a scheduled check rather than trusted to judgement:

```python
# scripts/check_sunset_readiness.py
"""Fails if a route scheduled for removal still has usage. Run in the release
pipeline of any change that removes a deprecated surface."""
THIRTY_DAYS = 'sum(increase(deprecated_endpoint_requests_total{route="%s"}[30d])) by (api_key_id)'

def assert_safe_to_remove(route: str) -> None:
    still_calling = prometheus.query(THIRTY_DAYS % route)
    if still_calling:
        keys = ", ".join(f"{s.labels['api_key_id']} ({int(s.value)} calls)"
                         for s in still_calling)
        raise SystemExit(
            f"REFUSING to remove {route}: still in use by {len(still_calling)} key(s): {keys}. "
            "Contact them and extend the sunset (02.13)."
        )
```

## Design Patterns / Tradeoffs

### Removal strategy

**A. Hard cutoff on an announced date**

- *Advantages:* predictable; bounded maintenance; forces migration.
- *Disadvantages:* breaks whoever did not migrate, all at once, usually outside business hours; the
  blast radius is unknown if you lack telemetry.
- *Failure mode:* the incident below.
- *Use when:* every consumer is internal and you can verify migration directly.

**B. Announcement + telemetry + brownouts + evidence gate** (✅ recommended)

- *Advantages:* the failure happens at a time you chose, with staff watching; unknown clients surface
  before removal; removal is verified rather than assumed.
- *Disadvantages:* longer programme; brownouts are deliberate customer-visible failures and need
  careful communication; requires telemetry you must build first.
- *Use when:* any external API.

**C. Never remove — support forever**

- *Advantages:* no client ever breaks.
- *Disadvantages:* every future change must accommodate the old surface; the cost compounds
  indefinitely; security fixes must be applied to code nobody understands any more.
- *Use when:* the surface is genuinely cheap to maintain (a read-only endpoint over stable data), or
  contractual obligations require it. **This is a legitimate choice** — make it explicitly, with the
  cost acknowledged, rather than by drift.

**D. Freeze — keep it working, refuse new consumers**

- *How it works:* existing keys continue; new API keys receive `404` for the deprecated surface.
- *Advantages:* the user population can only shrink; no client breaks; time is on your side.
- *Disadvantages:* two code paths persist; enforcement needs per-key gating.
- *Use when:* removal is not urgent but growth must stop. Underused, and often the right answer.

### Sunset date policy

| Consumers | Reasonable notice |
|---|---|
| Internal only, you own every caller | Weeks |
| First-party mobile apps | 2 app-store release cycles + the long tail of un-updated installs |
| Third-party integrators | 6–12 months |
| Enterprise with contractual change windows | 12–24 months, and check the contracts |

Whatever you choose, **be willing to move it**. A sunset date that slips because usage is non-zero is
a functioning process. A sunset date honoured while a customer is still calling is an outage you
scheduled for yourself.

## Production Scenario

**Ledgerly, Tuesday 09:00 UTC.** API v1 is removed, exactly as announced twelve months earlier and
reminded three times. The removal deploys cleanly. At 09:04, a P1 arrives from the second-largest
customer: their entire invoicing pipeline is down, and they are unable to issue invoices.

### Symptoms

- One customer's integration returns `404` on every request. Their invoicing has stopped completely.
- `deprecated_endpoint_requests_total` does not exist — v1 had no per-client telemetry.
- Ledgerly's aggregate v1 request rate before removal had been ~2,000/day against ~4 M/day on v2 —
  0.05%, which had been read as "effectively nobody".
- Over the next four hours, **six** customers report breakage. Three are on enterprise contracts.
- The customer's response to "we announced this twelve months ago" is, verifiably, that nobody at
  their company received it.

### Diagnosis

1. **Alert.** None fired. Removing an endpoint produces `404`s, and `404`s are normal background noise
   — nobody alerts on them. The signal was a support ticket.
2. **Metrics.** The aggregate v1 rate had been visible on a dashboard, and 0.05% looked like rounding
   error. What was not visible: **whose** 0.05%. 2,000 requests a day was one customer's entire
   invoicing pipeline.
3. **Logs.** Web-server logs (retained 30 days) can be re-read to reconstruct what telemetry should
   have captured:

```text
$ logcli query '{app="ledgerly-api"} | json | path=~"/v1/.*"' --since=720h \
    | jq -r .api_key_id | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn
   41102 key_live_2b8e…      ← one key, 94% of all v1 traffic
    1840 key_live_9c04…
     712 key_live_77f1…
      88 key_live_c318…
```

   One key was almost all of it. Had that been on a dashboard, the removal would never have shipped.

4. **Trace the announcements.** Emails went to the **billing contact** on each account — the address in
   the CRM. For the affected customer that was an accounts-payable mailbox. No technical contact was
   ever notified, and none was recorded.
5. **Check the client-visible signals.** The `Sunset` header had been emitted for twelve months. The
   customer's HTTP client discarded response headers it did not recognise, and nobody had ever looked.
   **A signal nobody consumes is not a warning.**
6. **Root cause is a process failure**, not a code failure. Every line of code behaved as designed.

### Root Cause

Removal was gated on a **date** rather than on **evidence**. Three specific gaps:

1. **No per-client usage telemetry.** Aggregate volume made a critical dependency look like noise.
   0.05% of traffic can be 100% of one customer's business process.
2. **Outreach went to the wrong people.** Billing contacts, not engineers. No technical contact field
   existed on the account model, so there was nobody else to email.
3. **No brownouts.** There was no moment before removal at which a silent client could surface. The
   first time anyone learned they had a problem was when their production stopped.

The `Sunset` headers were correct and ignored — which is the expected outcome for a header no tooling
of the customer's consumed. Headers are necessary and nowhere near sufficient.

### Fix

*Mitigation (12 minutes):* re-enable v1 behind a per-key allow-list containing only the six affected
keys. Their pipelines recovered on the next retry; nobody else regained access, so migration pressure
on everyone else was preserved.

*Permanent fix — a deprecation policy with an evidence gate:*

1. **Per-client usage telemetry on every deprecated surface**, from the moment of deprecation. The
   `deprecated()` decorator above is now mandatory and is enforced by a test that every route marked
   `deprecated=True` in OpenAPI also carries the decorator.
2. **A technical-contact field** on every account, collected at onboarding and required for API access.
   Deprecation notices go to billing **and** technical contacts, and to the email registered against
   each API key.
3. **Mandatory brownouts** — 15 minutes, 1 hour, 4 hours — at T-3, T-2 and T-1 months, each announced
   twice and run during business hours in the customer's primary timezone.
4. **The removal gate** (`check_sunset_readiness.py`) wired into the release pipeline, so a pull request
   that removes a deprecated route **fails CI** while usage is non-zero. This is the change that makes
   the policy real rather than aspirational: it is no longer possible to remove something in use, even
   deliberately, without an explicit override.
5. **In-product notification**: the dashboard shows a banner to admins of any tenant whose keys are
   calling a deprecated surface. The person who can act on it sees it where they already work.

### Prevention

- **The CI gate is the control.** Everything else — headers, emails, guides — informs; only the gate
  prevents.
- **A dashboard of deprecated usage by key, reviewed weekly** during any active deprecation. The review
  is a meeting agenda item, not a hope.
- **Alert on any deprecated surface with non-zero usage inside 60 days of sunset**, escalating to the
  account team rather than to engineering — because the remaining work is outreach, not code:

```yaml
- alert: DeprecatedEndpointStillInUseNearSunset
  expr: |
    sum by (route, api_key_id) (increase(deprecated_endpoint_requests_total[7d])) > 0
      and on() (sunset_days_remaining < 60)
  labels: {team: account-management}
  annotations:
    summary: "{{ $labels.api_key_id }} still calls {{ $labels.route }}, sunset in <60 days"
    runbook: "Contact the technical contact directly. Do not remove while this fires (02.13)."
```

- **Instrument *before* deprecating.** Telemetry added at removal time is useless; you need history.
  Ledgerly now adds usage counters to any surface it expects to deprecate within two years.
- **Treat 0.05% as a customer, not as noise.** The generalised lesson: aggregate percentages hide
  individual dependencies, and for a B2B product the individual is the unit that matters.

## Failure-First Checklist

| What if... | Answer |
|---|---|
| ...a client never sees the announcement? | Assume this. That is what brownouts and the evidence gate are for. |
| ...usage is non-zero on the sunset date? | Do not remove. Extend and contact who is left. The gate should make removal impossible. |
| ...a client ignores `Sunset` headers? | Most will. Headers inform tooling; they do not migrate anyone. |
| ...you remove and something breaks? | Have a per-key re-enable path ready before you remove, so mitigation is minutes not hours. |
| ...the traffic is 0.05% of the total? | That can be 100% of one customer's business process. Segment by client, always. |
| ...the technical contact left the company? | Route in-product notifications to tenant admins as well as email. |
| ...a deprecated endpoint has a security flaw? | Now you must patch code nobody owns — a real cost of choosing "support forever". |
| ...you deprecate an error `type` or a field rather than a route? | Same programme applies; you need per-field usage telemetry, which is harder — prefer to keep emitting it (02.9). |

## Common Pitfalls

**Removing on a date instead of on evidence.** The incident above. *Instead:* gate removal on usage
reaching zero, enforced in CI.

**Aggregate-only usage metrics.** 0.05% is invisible until it is somebody's whole business. *Instead:*
per-key, per-tenant counters.

**Announcing to billing contacts.** They are not the people with the code. *Instead:* technical
contacts, key-registered emails, in-product banners.

**Headers as the whole plan.** `Sunset` is machine-readable and almost universally unread. *Instead:*
headers **and** telemetry **and** outreach **and** brownouts.

**No brownouts.** The first failure then happens at removal, in production, for everyone at once.
*Instead:* escalating brownouts during business hours.

**`404` after removal.** Indistinguishable from a typo. *Instead:* `410 Gone` with a `Link` to the
migration guide.

**Deprecating without a successor.** Telling clients to stop without telling them what to do instead
guarantees they will not move. *Instead:* ship the replacement and the migration guide first.

**No rollback path.** If removal breaks someone, you need a per-key re-enable in minutes. *Instead:*
build the allow-list before you need it.

## How to Test This

- The `check_sunset_readiness.py` gate, wired into the release pipeline for any removal.
- A test that every route with `deprecated=True` in OpenAPI emits `Deprecation`, `Sunset` and `Link`
  headers, and records the usage counter.
- A test that a removed route returns `410` with a `Link`, not `404`.
- A brownout test: enable the flag and assert `410` with the correct problem body, then disable and
  assert normal service — verifying the abort path works before you need it.

## Interview Questions

1. *Explain.* Why do deprecation announcements usually fail? — They reach commercial contacts, not the
   engineers who own the integration, and nothing in the client's own tooling surfaces them.
2. *Explain.* What do `Deprecation` and `Sunset` do, and what are their limits? — Machine-readable
   markers; they inform tooling but migrate nobody.
3. *Design.* Plan the removal of API v1 with 200 integrators. — Successor and guide first, headers plus
   OpenAPI flags, per-key telemetry, targeted outreach, escalating brownouts, evidence gate.
4. *Debug this.* You removed an endpoint on the announced date and a major customer's production
   stopped. — No per-client telemetry; aggregate usage hid a single critical dependency.
5. *Explain.* Why `410` rather than `404` after removal? — `410` says "deliberately gone", pointing the
   engineer at a migration rather than a typo.
6. *Explain.* What is a brownout and what does it buy you? — A scheduled short outage that surfaces
   unknown clients while you are watching.
7. *Design.* Usage is non-zero the week before sunset. What do you do? — Do not remove; extend, contact
   the remaining keys, consider freezing new consumers.

## Key Takeaways

- **You cannot remove what you cannot measure.** Per-client usage telemetry is the prerequisite for the
  whole programme, and it must exist before you deprecate.
- The removal date is an **output** of the process, not an input. Gate removal on usage reaching zero,
  and enforce that gate in CI so it is not a matter of discipline.
- `Deprecation`, `Sunset`, `Link` and `deprecated: true` reach client *tooling* — necessary, and
  nowhere near sufficient on their own.
- Announce to **technical** contacts and in-product, not to billing addresses.
- **Brownouts** convert an unknown future failure into a controlled present one, with staff watching.
- Aggregate percentages lie. 0.05% of traffic is routinely 100% of one customer's business process.
- Return `410 Gone` with a migration link, and keep a per-key re-enable path ready before you remove.
- "Support it forever" and "freeze new consumers" are legitimate choices — make them explicitly, with
  the cost acknowledged.
