## Concept

### Plain-English Explanation

Every await in module 04 has been a promise to wait. This notebook is about taking the promise back. Two forces demand it: **deadlines** (the caller's patience is finite — the edge gives you 30 s, a user gives you less) and **abandonment** (the caller may be gone already). asyncio's answer to both is one mechanism, **cancellation**: a `CancelledError` thrown into a task *at its current await point*. Everything else — `asyncio.timeout`, `wait_for`, TaskGroup's sibling-cancel (03.8), graceful shutdown's drain deadline (03.14) — is this one mechanism wearing different schedules. And because it is an exception at an await, its limits are knowable in advance: it cannot land where there is no await (04.2's sync calls, 04.7's burns), it can be caught (and, catastrophically, swallowed — 03.9), and it arrives mid-operation, which makes *what your cleanup does* part of your correctness story.

### Technical Explanation

**Anatomy, captured.** Cancel a task parked at a sleep: the `CancelledError` raises *at that await*, the `finally` runs, the canceller sees the task cancelled. And the anti-pattern 03.9 outlawed, measured — a task that catches-and-continues:

```text
polite: CancelledError AT the await → finally ran → caller: task is cancelled
swallower: cancel() sent, waited 300ms — task finished? False
swallower: second cancel — done now? False
```

A cancel-swallowing loop survives *any number* of cancels; at 03.14's drain it is the task shutdown waits on until the grace period guillotines the process. Re-raise, always — cleanup goes in `finally`, never in the `except CancelledError` body's continuation.

**Timeouts are cancellation with a conversion at the border.** Inside an `asyncio.timeout(...)` block, expiry is delivered as `CancelledError` (your cleanup path is the same one); at the block's edge it becomes `TimeoutError` (the caller's verdict):

```text
inner : CancelledError (the mechanism is cancellation)
outer : TimeoutError  (converted at the block edge)
```

One mechanism, two faces — which is why code inside a deadline must treat `CancelledError` as "clean up and re-raise" without asking *why* (deadline? disconnect? sibling failure? the answer lives above you).

**What cancellation cannot reach.** A task "cancelled" while inside `run_in_executor`:

```text
await raised CancelledError at t=0.2s
...but the THREAD finished its work at t=1.0s anyway
```

The awaiter is freed; the sync work is a **zombie** — it completes, and its side effects land, with nobody waiting. 04.2's executor-wrapped SDK calls and 04.7's pool submissions all carry this asterisk: your timeout bounds *your waiting*, never *their working*. Bounding the work itself needs the work's own mechanisms (client timeouts inside the thread, 04.3; process termination, 04.7).

⚠️ **The discovery that amended 04.5.** What does cancelling a task do to the thing it is awaiting? The folklore says "nothing — you just stop waiting". Measured:

```text
naive await  : waiter cancelled -> flight.cancelled()=True   issued=[]
shielded     : waiter cancelled -> flight.cancelled()=False  issued=['token issued']
wait_for     : timeout          -> flight.cancelled()=True   issued=[]
```

**Cancelling a task cancels whatever future it is currently awaiting** — including a shared Task. 04.5's `TokenCache` as first written (`await self._refresh`) let one impatient waiter — a request whose own deadline expired — kill the refresh *for all thirty waiters*, converting one timeout into a herd-wide failure. The amendment (already applied to 04.5): waiters `await asyncio.shield(shared_task)` — the shield absorbs the waiter's cancellation, freeing the waiter while the flight lives on for everyone else. `wait_for`, by contrast, cancels what it wraps *on purpose*: it is a kill-switch for owned work, never for shared work.

### Mental Model

> Cancellation is a letter that can only be delivered to a mailbox (an await). Sync code has no mailbox — letters pile up outside while the work finishes. Catching the letter and not resigning (re-raising) makes you unfireable, which is worse than it sounds at shutdown. And the letter is *contagious downward*: firing a waiter fires whatever it waits on — unless that thing is shared, in which case you shield it, because one caller's impatience must never demolish common property. Deadlines, then, are just scheduled letters — and budgets are making sure the letters nest.

## How It Works

The budget arithmetic — deadlines must *nest*, and every layer's number derives from the one above:

```text
edge / LB timeout                        30s   (01.7's chain has the outermost say)
  └ handler budget (asyncio.timeout)     25s   ← must exist; uvicorn imposes NONE
      └ renderer call: per-try           10s   (04.3's read timeout)
          × (1 + retries)                 2 tries → 20s  ✓ fits
      └ tax call: per-try                 3s, 2 tries → 6s ✓
  spare for CPU slices, queue, jitter:    ~5s
RULE: Σ(per-try × tries) + slack < handler < edge — checked in CI, not in prod
```

Two structural facts make the middle line load-bearing. First, **uvicorn imposes no request deadline** — without an explicit handler budget, a request's lifetime is the sum of whatever its dependencies allow, which under retry multiplication exceeds any edge timeout. Second, **the edge timing out does not stop you** — captured on the pinned stack, a client that hangs up at t=0.3 s of a 1.5 s handler:

```text
completed side effects after both disconnects: {'blind': 1, 'aware': 0}
```

The abandoned handler **runs to completion** — no cancellation on disconnect; the response is written to a dead socket. (The `aware` variant polled `request.is_disconnected()` and aborted.) This default is a mercy — no CancelledError erupting mid-commit because wifi blinked — and a bill: after an edge timeout, the *real* work continues, invisible, holding 04.3's pools, while the client's retry arrives to run the same work *again*. Work-after-abandonment is capacity spent on nobody, and under dependency brownout it is the amplifier: each edge timeout adds a retry's load *without removing the original's*.

```text
   renderer brownout, no handler budget, edge=30s:
   t=0    request A: render (10s try #1) ──────────┐
   t=10   try #2 ─────────────────────────┐        │  A's work continues
   t=20   try #3 ─────────────────┐       │        │  (nobody told it to stop)
   t=30   EDGE: 504 to client ────┼───────┼────────┤
   t=30   client retries → request A' joins the queue BEHIND A's zombie tries
          fleet load = live requests + zombies: 2× at one retry, then 3×…
```

## Code Example

The handler budget — one middleware line that gives every request a deadline the process actually enforces:

```python
# ledgerly/middleware/deadline.py (registered with 03.7's stack)
class DeadlineMiddleware:
    """Every request gets a budget strictly below the edge timeout. Expiry
    cancels the handler AT ITS CURRENT AWAIT; 03.8's handlers turn the
    TimeoutError into a 504 problem while we still own a live socket."""

    def __init__(self, app: ASGIApp, budget: float = 25.0) -> None:
        self.app, self.budget = app, budget

    async def __call__(self, scope, receive, send) -> None:
        if scope["type"] != "http":
            return await self.app(scope, receive, send)
        try:
            async with asyncio.timeout(self.budget):
                await self.app(scope, receive, send)
        except TimeoutError:
            raise DeadlineExceeded(budget=self.budget)   # → 504 problem (02.9)
```

And the retry helper that makes the ladder budget-aware — the sentence "retries without a budget make outages worse" (§7, promised since module 01) finally becomes code:

```python
# ledgerly/clients/retry.py (15.14 grows this into the full ladder)
async def with_retries[T](op: Callable[[], Awaitable[T]], *, tries: int,
                          per_try: float, budget: float) -> T:
    """Retries spend from a BUDGET, not from hope: each try's timeout is the
    smaller of per_try and what remains; no budget left = no try happens."""
    deadline = asyncio.get_running_loop().time() + budget
    last_exc: Exception | None = None
    for attempt in range(tries):
        remaining = deadline - asyncio.get_running_loop().time()
        if remaining <= 0.05:
            break                                        # don't start doomed work
        try:
            async with asyncio.timeout(min(per_try, remaining)):
                return await op()
        except (TimeoutError, httpx.TransportError) as exc:
            last_exc = exc
            await asyncio.sleep(backoff_with_jitter(attempt, remaining))
    raise RetriesExhausted(budget=budget) from last_exc
```

The `remaining <= 0.05` guard is the arithmetic made local: a try that cannot fit in the remaining budget is not attempted, so the multiplication (`tries × per_try`) can never exceed what the caller granted — whatever anyone later sets `tries` to. Finally, the disconnect-awareness pattern for genuinely expensive work (measured: it is opt-in on this stack):

```python
@router.post("/v1/invoices/{invoice_id}/preview-pdf")
async def preview_pdf(invoice_id: InvoiceId, request: Request, svc: Service) -> Response:
    pdf_task = asyncio.create_task(svc.render_preview(invoice_id))
    while not pdf_task.done():                      # a PREVIEW serves only the
        if await request.is_disconnected():         # eyes that asked for it:
            pdf_task.cancel()                       # caller gone → stop paying
            raise ClientDisconnected
        await asyncio.wait({pdf_task}, timeout=0.25)
    return Response(pdf_task.result(), media_type="application/pdf")
```

The choice is deliberate per endpoint: previews cancel on disconnect (worthless without a reader); the *send* path never does (the send is real regardless of who is watching — its integrity comes from 04.6's transactions, and duplicates are 02.8's key's job when the client retries).

> **Version note** — captured on the pinned stack (uvicorn 0.46 + Starlette 1.0): a client disconnect does **not** cancel a running HTTP handler; `request.is_disconnected()` is the opt-in. This is server-implementation behaviour, not a Python guarantee — re-run `_lab/lab_04_08_cancellation.py` section E after upgrading uvicorn/Starlette before relying on it either way. `asyncio.timeout` semantics (CancelledError inside, TimeoutError at the edge) are 3.11+; the awaiting-task cancellation propagation (section D) is core asyncio and version-stable.

## Design Patterns / Tradeoffs

### Protecting work that must not be half-done

- **Transactions (04.6)** — cancellation mid-request aborts the transaction; the outbox row vanishes with it; the loser ships nothing. *Advantages:* correct by construction, no special-casing cancellation at all. *The default:* if the critical section is database-shaped, the database already solves this.
- **`asyncio.shield`** — for the rare *non-transactional* critical await (the shared flight; an external commit that has no rollback): the waiter's deadline detaches from the work's fate. *Disadvantages:* shielded work outlives its request unobserved (who handles its failure now?); shields stack badly (a shield inside a shield inside a timeout is unreadable); ⚠️ shield does not survive *loop shutdown*. *Use when:* shared awaits (04.5's amendment) and last-resort atomicity; never as a habit.
- **Hand the work to an owner that outlives requests** — 03.9's supervised task or 03.10's outbox/worker: the request *records the intention* transactionally and returns; nobody's disconnect can touch work that was never theirs. *Use when:* the work matters beyond the caller — which is most work that tempted you to shield it.

### Where the deadline should live

Per-call only (04.3's four phases): precise but sums unpredictably under retries and fan-out. Handler-level only: bounds the total but converts every internal overrun into an undifferentiated 504. **Both, nested** — per-try timeouts spending from a handler budget under an edge ceiling — is the answer the arithmetic forces: the outer bound is the caller's contract, the inner ones are diagnosis (04.3's taxonomy: *which* phase died names the culprit). **Recommendation:** the ladder budget-aware everywhere (`with_retries`), `DeadlineMiddleware` at 25 s under a 30 s edge, per-endpoint overrides only downward — and disconnect-cancellation opted into per route, on the "does this work serve anyone but the caller?" test.

## Production Scenario

**Ledgerly, Tuesday 24 November, 13:05.** The PDF renderer browns out again — not down, just slow: renders that took 2 s take 25–40 s. (20 October's bulkheads mean tax calls are untouched this time — the containment held.) By **13:12**, send-invoice is failing at the edge with 504s, and the api fleet's renderer-client pools sit at 100% with `PoolTimeout`s rising. By **13:20**, replicas are working flat-out while *completing almost nothing anyone receives*.

### Symptoms

- Edge: 504s on `/v1/invoices/{id}/send` (the LB's 30 s), and rising request *rates* on the same route — clients retrying per their 01.3 semantics.
- The renderer client pool (04.3's metrics): in-use pinned at its cap of 8 per replica, queue full, `PoolTimeout`s — but renderer *completions* keep trickling in at 25–40 s each… for requests whose edge verdict was delivered minutes ago.
- **The zombie ratio** — responses the app finished vs responses the LB delivered: normally 1.00, now **2.7** — the fleet computes almost three sends for every one a client receives. (The 03.13/03.14 absence-family metric, in its most literal form: work with no witness.)
- Loop lag flat, no freezes, DB healthy: the process is *working correctly* on work nobody wants.

### Diagnosis

1. **Alert → Metrics.** 504s at the edge with healthy loops and a saturated single-dependency pool reads at first like 20 October — but the zombie ratio is new information: the pool isn't merely full, it is full of *the past*. Requests the edge already 504'd are still inside, on their second and third renderer tries.
2. **Read the timeline of one request** (traces): try #1 10 s (04.3's read timeout), backoff, try #2, backoff, try #3 — the retry helper predates the budget rule: `tries=3` was set during the 20 Oct follow-up hardening, and 3 × 10 s + backoffs ≈ 34 s > the 30 s edge. Every slow-renderer request is *guaranteed* to outlive its caller.
3. **And nothing stops it** — the lab's E capture is the mechanism: disconnects don't cancel handlers on this stack, and there is no handler budget; uvicorn happily runs the 34 s to completion, holding a pool loan (03.11) the whole way, then writes the response to a dead socket.
4. **The amplification closes the loop:** each edge 504 spawns a client retry that queues behind the zombie holding the pool — measured fleet math: live × (1 + zombie ratio) ≈ 3.7× renderer demand against a renderer already drowning. The retries are making the outage worse — the §7 sentence, playing out on the dashboard.

### Root Cause

Per-call timeouts and retry counts were each locally reasonable but never summed against any owning deadline — 3 tries × 10 s exceeded the 30 s edge, no handler budget existed to cancel overrunning work, and disconnect does not cancel handlers — so under renderer brownout every request was structurally condemned to outlive its caller, converting client retries into pure load amplification (zombie ratio 2.7) against the degraded dependency.

### Fix

*Mitigation (13:26):* config: renderer `tries` 3 → 1 and the per-tenant send-concurrency cap from 20 Oct halved. Zombies drain within a minute of their single try; the pool frees; sends fail *fast* with the `upstream-capacity` problem + `Retry-After: 120` (02.9/04.3), which is honest — the renderer *is* degraded — and stops the amplification. Renders recover as the provider stabilises at 14:0x.

*Permanent fix:* `DeadlineMiddleware` (25 s under the 30 s edge) so no request can outlive its caller by more than the conversion window; `with_retries` with the budget parameter replacing every hand-rolled retry loop — tries now spend from the handler's remaining time, making the 20 Oct-style "just add retries" hardening arithmetically incapable of exceeding the edge again.

### Prevention

- **The incident closes into a named CI test** — the nesting checked where configs live, not where outages happen:

```python
# tests/contract/test_deadline_budgets_nest.py
def test_every_route_budget_fits_inside_the_edge(app_state, settings) -> None:
    """3 tries x 10s outlived the 30s edge and every caller with it (24 Nov).
    Per-try x tries + slack must fit the handler budget; the handler budget
    must fit the edge. Checked in CI, enforced by with_retries at runtime (04.8)."""
    assert settings.handler_budget < settings.edge_timeout - 2.0
    for dep, policy in app_state["retry_policies"].items():
        worst = policy.tries * policy.per_try + policy.max_backoff_total
        assert worst < settings.handler_budget, \
            f"{dep}: worst case {worst}s cannot fit the {settings.handler_budget}s budget"
```

- **The zombie ratio becomes a standing metric with an alert** — app-completed ÷ edge-delivered > 1.2 pages: capacity serving nobody is an outage-in-progress even while every individual component reads healthy.
- The runbook's brownout page gains the rule of thumb: under dependency degradation, *reduce* tries first — a retry against a drowning dependency is a donation.

## Failure-First Checklist

| What if... | Answer |
|---|---|
| ...a task is cancelled at an await? | CancelledError raises there; `finally` runs; re-raise always (measured: a swallower survives every cancel and hangs 03.14's drain). |
| ...a timeout expires mid-block? | Inside: CancelledError (clean up as usual). At the block edge: TimeoutError (the verdict). Don't guess *why* you were cancelled below the boundary. |
| ...the cancelled task was awaiting a shared task? | ⚠️ The shared task dies with it (measured) — unless waiters shield. Audit every `await` on common property (caches, flights, broadcasts). |
| ...the work is inside a sync call? | Untouchable: the awaiter frees, the thread/process finishes and its side effects land (measured). Bound the work with its own mechanisms (04.3 client timeouts, pool termination). |
| ...the client disconnects mid-handler? | On this stack: nothing — the handler completes into a dead socket (measured). Opt into `is_disconnected()` where the work serves only the caller. |
| ...retries are added to a call? | They multiply: per-try × tries must re-fit the handler budget — `with_retries` enforces it at runtime, the CI test at merge time. |
| ...the edge times out but the app doesn't? | A zombie: pool loans held, work witnessed by nobody, the client's retry doubling load. The handler budget exists precisely to make this window small. |
| ...cancellation lands mid-transaction? | The abort is the cleanup (04.6): the outbox row vanishes, the loser ships nothing. Transactional critical sections need no shield. |
| ...shutdown (03.14) meets all of the above? | Drain = mass cancellation: swallowers hang it, shields outlive it only until the guillotine, zombies die with the process. Every rule here is also a shutdown rule. |

## Common Pitfalls

**Swallowing CancelledError.** The unfireable task: survives every cancel, hangs every drain. *Instead:* cleanup in `finally`; the except block re-raises, no exceptions to the exception.

**Awaiting shared tasks nakedly.** One impatient waiter demolishes the flight for thirty (measured; it shipped in 04.5's first cut). *Instead:* `shield` on every await of common property; `wait_for` only for work you own outright.

**Timeouts as decoration.** A per-call timeout without an owning budget just schedules *when* the sum exceeds the edge. *Instead:* nested budgets — edge > handler > Σ(tries × per-try) — checked in CI.

**Retry counts as a hardening knob.** `tries=3` added in a calm review became 3× load against a drowning dependency and 34 s of zombie work per request. *Instead:* retries spend from a budget; under brownout, spend less.

**Assuming disconnect cancels you** (or assuming it doesn't, after an upgrade). It's server behaviour, not physics. *Instead:* the lab's E section is a one-run answer per stack version; choose awareness per endpoint.

**Shield as a habit.** Unowned failures, unreadable nesting, false safety at shutdown. *Instead:* transactions for database-shaped atomicity; ownership transfer (outbox/supervised task) for work that matters beyond the caller; shield for the shortlist that remains.

## Interview Questions

1. *Explain.* What is cancellation in asyncio, mechanically, and where can it not act? — A CancelledError injected at the task's current await; no await, no injection: sync calls, executor threads and finished code are unreachable — the awaiter frees while the work zombies on.
2. *Debug this.* Under a dependency brownout, edge 504s rise, the dependency's pool is pinned, and the app completes 2.7 responses per response delivered. — Retries × per-try exceed the edge with no handler budget and no disconnect cancellation: zombies hold the pool while client retries amplify load. Cut tries now; install nested budgets permanently.
3. *Explain.* `asyncio.timeout` vs `wait_for` vs `shield` — one sentence each, and the trap in each. — timeout: deadline as scoped cancel (inside sees CancelledError); wait_for: kills the task it wraps (never wrap shared work); shield: detaches waiter deadline from work fate (who owns the failure now?).
4. *Explain.* Why did cancelling one API request break token refresh for every concurrent request, and what's the fix? — Cancelling a task cancels the future it awaits — the shared flight died with its waiter. Waiters `await asyncio.shield(flight)`; the flight's lifecycle belongs to the cache, not to any caller.
5. *Design.* Deadline architecture for a handler calling two dependencies with retries, behind a 30 s edge. — Handler budget 25 s via middleware; per-dependency per-try from 04.3; a budget-spending retry helper so Σ fits by construction; CI asserts the nesting; 504/problem conversion while the socket is alive.
6. *Explain.* The client disconnected — should your handler stop? — Depends what the work serves: caller-only work (previews) should opt into `is_disconnected` and stop; real-world work (sends, payments) must complete under transactional protection, with idempotency keys absorbing the retry. And know your stack's default: measured, not assumed.
7. *Design.* Make "retries without a budget make outages worse" enforceable, not aspirational. — Budget parameter in the one shared retry helper (remaining-time-capped tries, jittered backoff, no doomed starts), a CI nesting test over the policy registry, and a zombie-ratio alert as the runtime tripwire.

## Key Takeaways

- Cancellation is one mechanism — an exception at an await — behind every timeout, drain and sibling-cancel; its cleanup path is `finally`, and re-raising is non-negotiable (a swallower survives every cancel, measured).
- ⚠️ Cancelling a task cancels what it's awaiting: naked awaits on shared tasks let one caller's deadline demolish common property — shield the waiters (04.5 stands amended by measurement).
- Sync work is beyond reach: your timeout bounds your waiting, never their working — zombies complete and their side effects land (measured).
- On this stack, disconnects cancel nothing: abandoned handlers run to completion into dead sockets — awareness is per-endpoint opt-in, chosen by who the work serves.
- Deadlines must nest — edge > handler > Σ(per-try × tries) — and the handler budget must *exist*, because uvicorn imposes none; check the arithmetic in CI, enforce it in the retry helper.
- Retries spend from a budget or they amplify outages: under brownout, fewer tries is the mitigation, honest fast failure (503/504 + Retry-After) the interface.
- Half-done protection in order of preference: transactions (04.6), ownership transfer (outbox/supervised task), and only then shield — briefly, and knowing shutdown out-ranks it.
- Watch the zombie ratio (app-completed ÷ edge-delivered): capacity serving nobody is the invisible outage, and 1.0 is the only healthy number.
